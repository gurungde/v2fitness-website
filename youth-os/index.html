The big WHY

Chronological age does not tell the whole development story.

Two children of the same age can differ substantially in biological maturation, training experience, movement skill and readiness. A smaller version of an adult program is therefore not automatically an appropriate youth program.

🦴
Growing structures are still developing

Bones, growth plates, muscles and tendons do not mature at identical rates.

🧠
Movement can temporarily feel different

Rapid changes in limb length and body proportions may affect coordination and technique.

📊
Training load needs context

Growth information should sit alongside health, pain, sleep, training history, skill and workload—not replace them.

🌱
Development is individual

The goal is not to label a child, but to support appropriate challenge, confidence and long-term participation.

Peak Height Velocity

Why understanding the growth spurt can matter

PHV describes the period of fastest height growth during adolescence. An anthropometric equation can estimate time before or after PHV, but it is an approximation—not a direct measurement of puberty or injury risk.

Stage 1
🌱

Steady growth

Build movement variety, enjoyment, technique and broad physical literacy.

Stage 2
↗️

Growth accelerating

Watch changing movement quality, recovery and emerging discomfort.

Stage 3
📈

Around PHV

Prioritise technique, communication and sensible workload adjustments where needed.

Stage 4
🏋️

Post-PHV

Progress strength and power according to competency, health and training age.

Always
👂

Individual monitoring

Pain, fatigue, movement, sleep and enjoyment remain more important than one estimate.

Important counterpoint: Knowing estimated PHV does not automatically prevent injury and should never be used as the sole basis for reducing or increasing load. Its value is as one additional conversation and monitoring signal within a child-centred coaching process.
Designed for families and coaches

Turn curiosity into better questions

Youth OS is intended to help adults notice, discuss and monitor development—not diagnose a child or produce a rigid training prescription.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

For parents

Understand why a child’s movement, confidence or training response may change as they grow.

  • See adult height as a range, not a promise
  • Learn what PHV and maturity offset mean
  • Know when pain or growth concerns need professional review
  • Use results to start a better conversation with coaches
🧑‍🏫

For coaches

Add growth context to observation, communication, session design and workload monitoring.

  • Avoid treating same-age athletes as biologically identical
  • Reinforce technique and movement competency
  • Monitor rapid growth, pain, fatigue and coordination together
  • Individualise challenge without undertraining young people

Feature 01

Adult Height Estimator

Uses the Khamis–Roche method with current height, weight and biological-parent heights to estimate adult stature without a bone-age X-ray.

  • Shows an estimate and expected range
  • Explains method limitations and population assumptions
  • Does not predict athletic ability or health
Use “estimate,” not “prediction,” in claims. Final height can differ because of genetics, health, nutrition, measurement error and individual growth patterns.
Feature 02

PHV / Maturity-Offset Estimator

Uses the Mirwald anthropometric equation to estimate years before or after PHV from age, standing height, sitting height and weight.

  • Introduces possible timing relative to the growth spurt
  • Explains general training and monitoring considerations
  • Encourages repeated measurement rather than one-off labelling
The Mirwald equation has known individual and population limitations. It is not a clinical maturity assessment and should not independently determine training load.

Clear boundaries

Educational—not medical or prescriptive

Strong disclaimers support transparency, but they do not replace safe design, accurate claims, appropriate consent or professional responsibility.

Medical disclaimer

V2Fitness and V2 Youth OS do not provide medical advice, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, injury clearance or return-to-sport decisions. Consult an appropriately qualified health professional for concerns about growth, puberty, pain, injury, nutrition or development.

Estimate disclaimer

Adult-height and maturity-offset outputs are statistical estimates based on user-entered measurements and published equations. They are not guarantees, direct measures of biological maturity or indicators of future athletic potential.

Training disclaimer

General training considerations are educational only. Program design must account for health, pain, movement competency, training age, sport demands, workload, supervision, sleep, recovery and the individual child.

Risk disclaimer

PHV does not itself prove that a child is injured or will become injured. The estimate should not be used alone to change training, select talent, exclude participation or delay appropriate clinical assessment.

Research foundations: Khamis & Roche, Predicting adult stature without using skeletal age; Mirwald et al., An assessment of maturity from anthropometric measurements; IOC, Consensus statement on youth athletic development.
